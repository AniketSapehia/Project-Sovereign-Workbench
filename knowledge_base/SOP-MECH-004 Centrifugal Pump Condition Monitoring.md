# SOP-MECH-004: Condition Monitoring of Centrifugal Pumps

Revision: 3 | Effective date: 01-04-2026 | Owner: Mechanical Maintenance Department

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines how the vibration and bearing temperature of centrifugal pumps are measured, how readings are classified, and what action must be taken when limits are exceeded.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all centrifugal pumps with driver rating between 15 kW and 300 kW in process units, offsites and utilities. Pumps above 300 kW are covered by SOP-MECH-005.

## 3. Responsibilities

The Condition Monitoring (CM) Engineer collects readings, enters them in the CM register and raises a notification when a limit is exceeded. The Area Maintenance Engineer plans the corrective work. The Unit Shift In-charge must be informed immediately of any Zone D reading.

## 4. Measurement Frequency

Critical pumps (marked "C" in the equipment register): readings every 15 days.
Essential pumps (marked "E"): readings every 30 days.
General pumps (marked "G"): readings every 60 days.
After any overhaul, readings must be taken within 24 hours of restart and again after 7 days.

## 5. Vibration Severity Zones

Overall vibration velocity is measured in mm/s RMS on both the drive-end (DE) and non-drive-end (NDE) bearing housings, in horizontal, vertical and axial directions. The HIGHEST of all readings is used for classification.

Zone A (up to 2.8 mm/s): Good. Newly commissioned machines normally fall in this zone. No action.
Zone B (above 2.8 and up to 4.5 mm/s): Acceptable for unrestricted long-term operation. Continue normal monitoring.
Zone C (above 4.5 and up to 7.1 mm/s): Not acceptable for long-term operation. Increase monitoring to weekly, carry out spectrum analysis to find the cause, and plan corrective maintenance within 30 days.
Zone D (above 7.1 mm/s): Danger. Vibration may cause damage. Inform the Shift In-charge immediately, change over to the standby pump, and stop the pump within 24 hours.

## 6. Bearing Temperature Limits

Bearing housing temperature is measured with a calibrated contact thermometer or infrared gun.

Normal: up to 80 °C.
Alarm: above 80 °C and up to 90 °C. Check lubrication (oil level, oil condition, grease quantity) and re-measure within 8 hours.
Trip / stop: above 90 °C. Change over to the standby pump and stop the pump.

A temperature rise of more than 15 °C compared with the previous reading at similar load must be investigated even if the absolute value is below 80 °C.

## 7. Corrective Actions

Common causes of high vibration are unbalance, misalignment, bearing wear, looseness, cavitation and pipe strain. The spectrum analysis report must identify the most likely cause before parts are ordered. Bearing replacement must be done together with a laser alignment check of the pump-motor coupling. After any corrective work the pump must return to Zone A or Zone B.

## 8. Records

All readings, spectrum reports and corrective actions are recorded in the CM register and in the maintenance management system against the equipment tag number. Records are retained for 10 years.
